# Case Study — Decision Making Under Uncertainty

## Problem
Many business decisions cannot be modeled responsibly with one deterministic forecast. This portfolio shows how probability, risk preference, information and simulation change decisions.

## Decision trees and sensitivity
Expected Monetary Value identifies the best decision under stated probabilities. Sensitivity analysis then asks when that recommendation changes. In the arcade exercise, the preferred game changed when Game 1's payoff crossed roughly 30.5.

## Value of information
The StellarPath example quantified the economic value of reducing uncertainty: approximately $2.75M. A separate Bayesian example found only ~$81.5K of information value, below a $100K information cost.

## Risk aversion
Expected value and certainty equivalent answer different questions. In one exercise, risk aversion reduced the value of information to roughly $2.2K before a $100K agency cost, reinforcing that information is only valuable when it can change decisions enough to justify its price.

## Monte Carlo
Simulation extends the framework to continuous and correlated uncertainty. Repeated random draws create full outcome distributions, allowing expected value, percentiles, probability of loss and other risk measures to be estimated.

## Why it matters
The project demonstrates prescriptive analytics under uncertainty: not merely predicting outcomes, but quantifying uncertainty and choosing actions consistent with risk and information costs.
